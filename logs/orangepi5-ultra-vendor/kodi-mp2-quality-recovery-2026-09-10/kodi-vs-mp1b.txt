Kodi DRMPRIME vs the proven MP1b raw playback probe
===================================================

Purpose: settle whether the washed-out HDR appearance the operator reported
is something Kodi does, or something this display does.

Held constant
  file        /var/tmp/mp1b/past-lives.mkv
  sha256      f4e32b8d3feb7efdebaaefaf7338a25bcc86ee144bf82bd4b93da6b80906f96a
  timestamp   30:00 in both runs
  sink        BenQ RD280UG, same input, same picture mode, untouched between
              the two runs
  decoder     hevc_rkmpp in both
  gap         under two minutes; Kodi stopped, probe started

A = Kodi 22.0b2-Piers, GBM/DRM standalone, DRMPRIME direct-to-plane,
    hardware Mali GUI, this gate's patch set
B = tools/hdr-playback-probe, the Gate MP1b binary. No Kodi, no GUI plane,
    no compositor: decoder straight onto a DRM plane.

Reading
  The reference path and Kodi are indistinguishable, measured and seen. Kodi
  is therefore NOT a regression against the MP1b baseline: it reproduces it.

  The washed-out HDR10 appearance belongs to the sink. This is a BenQ RD280UG,
  a 28" productivity monitor, not the Sony BRAVIA KD-65XE9005 that Gates
  MP1a/MP1b were judged on. Its EDID advertises PQ and BT.2020 and it reports
  itself in HDR mode, but advertising ST2084 and rendering it well are
  different things: a monitor of this class has neither the peak luminance nor
  the tone mapping to make a PQ grade look right, and PQ shown flat is exactly
  "washed out".

  Nothing in this gate can be concluded about HDR *picture quality* from this
  display. What can be concluded, and is, is that the HDR *signal* is correct
  and identical to the proven reference. Re-judging picture quality needs the
  Sony back on the input; that is a sink swap, not a code change.

  FAULT DOMAIN: sink (BenQ RD280UG HDR10 rendering), not the RK3588 pipeline
  and not Kodi.
